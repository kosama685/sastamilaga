Visa guide

Visit and tourist visa: complete step-by-step guide

A visit visa lets you travel for tourism, to see family or friends, or for a short private trip. You cannot work on it. Most refusals come from weak evidence of funds or of your reasons to return home, so this guide focuses on what decides the outcome.

Step by step

  1. 1

    Check what your passport needs

    Look up the destination: visa-free, visa on arrival, e-visa, electronic travel authorisation, or an embassy visa. Always confirm on the official government portal before booking.

  2. 2

    Check your passport

    Most countries want at least six months of validity beyond your return date and blank visa pages. Renew first if you are close.

  3. 3

    Plan the trip on paper

    Decide dates, cities and where you will stay. Keep refundable hotel bookings and a flight reservation rather than paid tickets until the visa is issued.

  4. 4

    Collect your documents

    Use the checklist below. Every document should tell the same story: who you are, why you are going, who pays and why you will come back.

  5. 5

    Apply on the official channel

    Apply on the government e-visa portal, through the embassy, or at the official visa application centre. Book the appointment early in peak season.

  6. 6

    Give biometrics and attend any interview

    Bring originals of everything you uploaded. Answer briefly and honestly; your answers must match your documents.

  7. 7

    Track, collect and check the visa

    When the visa arrives, check names, passport number, dates, number of entries and permitted length of stay before you travel.

Documents you will usually need

  • Passport valid for six months or more, with blank pages, plus copies of old passports and visas
  • Recent photographs to the destination's size and background rules
  • Completed application form and fee receipt
  • Bank statements (often the last six months) with a balance that fits the trip
  • Employment letter with leave approval, or business registration and tax returns if self-employed
  • Hotel booking or the host's invitation letter and a copy of their ID or residence permit
  • Return or onward flight reservation and a day-by-day itinerary
  • Travel medical insurance covering the whole stay (mandatory for Schengen countries)
  • Family Registration Certificate (FRC) from NADRA when family members travel together
  • Polio vaccination certificate, required for travellers leaving Pakistan

Tips and warnings

  • Explain any large recent deposit in your bank account; unexplained funds are a common reason for refusal.
  • Show ties to Pakistan: a job, a business, property, family or studies you will return to.
  • Never submit fake bookings, bank letters or invitations. A finding of deception can lead to a long ban.
  • A previous refusal must be declared. Fix the reason before you apply again.

Frequently asked questions

Can I work on a visit visa?

No. Visit visas do not allow paid work. Working without permission can lead to removal and a ban.

How much bank balance do I need?

Most countries do not publish one fixed figure. Your funds should comfortably cover flights, accommodation and daily costs for the whole stay, and be consistent with your income.

Can a visit visa be converted to a work visa?

Usually not from inside the country. Most countries require you to apply for a work visa from home. A few allow a change of status; check the official rules.

How early should I apply?

As early as the destination allows. Many embassies accept applications months ahead, and appointments fill quickly in summer and around holidays.
