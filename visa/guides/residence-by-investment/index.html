Residence by investment: golden visas explained

Residence-by-investment programmes, often called golden visas, give you the right to live in a country in return for a qualifying investment such as property, a business, government bonds or an investment fund. Residence is not citizenship: you keep your passport, and citizenship usually requires years of residence later.

Step by step

  1. 1

    Decide what you need

    Right to live, visa-free travel in a region, education for children, or a path to permanent residence or citizenship. Programmes differ widely.

  2. 2

    Shortlist programmes and check status

    Programmes open, change and close often. Confirm the current rules on the government's official site.

  3. 3

    Check the source of your funds

    Every programme runs due diligence. You must document where the investment money came from.

  4. 4

    Choose a licensed adviser and lawyer

    Use advisers authorised by the programme where that applies. Never pay cash to an unverified agent.

  5. 5

    Make the investment and apply

    Usually in that order or in parallel; follow the official sequence.

  6. 6

    Biometrics, approval and renewal

    Keep the investment for the required period and meet any minimum stay rules to renew.

Documents you will usually need

  • Passport and civil documents for every family member
  • Police clearance certificates
  • Source-of-funds evidence: tax returns, sale deeds, business accounts, bank records
  • Investment documents (title deed, fund certificate, company registration)
  • Health insurance
  • Proof of address

Tips and warnings

  • Several well-known programmes have closed or changed since 2022 (for example the UK investor visa, Ireland's IIP, Spain's golden visa and the real-estate route in Portugal). Never rely on an old article.
  • Ask whether the residence leads to permanent residence or citizenship, and after how many years.
  • Count every cost: government fees, due diligence, legal fees, taxes and the cost of holding the investment.

Frequently asked questions

Is residence by investment the same as citizenship?

No. It gives you residence. Citizenship, where available, usually needs years of residence, language and integration tests.

Can my family be included?

Most programmes allow a spouse and dependent children, and some allow parents. Each person passes due diligence.
