Business visa: how to apply for meetings, conferences and trade fairs

A business visa covers short business activities such as meetings, negotiations, conferences, trade fairs and site visits. It does not allow you to be employed or paid by an organisation in the destination country.

Ask our team Choose a country

Step by step

  1. 1

    Confirm the activity is allowed

    Meetings, conferences and negotiations are usually allowed. Hands-on work, installation or being paid locally usually needs a work visa.

  2. 2

    Get an invitation letter

    From the host company or event organiser, on letterhead, with dates, purpose, who pays and contact details.

  3. 3

    Prepare company documents

    Your employer's letter, company registration, tax registration and evidence of the business relationship.

  4. 4

    Apply on the official channel

    E-visa portal, embassy or official visa centre, depending on the country.

  5. 5

    Attend biometrics or interview

    Be ready to explain the business purpose in plain words.

  6. 6

    Travel within the permitted dates

    Keep the invitation and company letter with you at the border.

Documents you will usually need

  • Passport valid for six months or more
  • Invitation letter from the host company or event
  • Employer's cover letter confirming your job, salary and the trip's purpose
  • Company registration and tax documents (if you own the business)
  • Bank statements and evidence of who pays for the trip
  • Conference registration or trade fair badge, if relevant
  • Travel insurance and accommodation details

Tips and warnings

  • Keep the purpose consistent across the invitation, the cover letter and the form.
  • A history of business trips and returns strengthens later applications.
  • If you will train staff, install equipment or be paid locally, ask whether you need a work permit instead.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sign contracts on a business visa?

Usually yes; negotiating and signing contracts is a typical business-visitor activity. Check the destination's list of permitted activities.

Can my company pay me while I travel?

Yes, if your salary is paid by your employer at home. Being paid by a company in the destination usually needs a work visa.
